# Entry and visa requirements for Colombian passport holders cycling overland Honduras → El Salvador → Guatemala (→ Belize) → Mexico (situation as of October 2026)

> **Method note (read first):** All findings below come from web-search result summaries gathered on 2026-10-17. Direct page fetches were not possible in this session: WebFetch failed with DNS errors on every host, and the egress proxy refused direct downloads. The session's web-search quota then ran out, so a few planned checks were never run (Nicaragua status for Colombians, INM opening hours at Talismán and Ciudad Hidalgo). Each claim cites the page the search engine attributed it to. Where a search summary did not show a publication date, the note says "date not shown". ## 1. Honduras, El Salvador, Guatemala: do Colombians need a visa in 2026, how long can they stay, and how does the CA-4 90-day stay and its extension work?

### Takeaway
Colombians do not need a visa for tourism in Guatemala, El Salvador or Honduras in 2026. They enter with a passport, ideally valid for at least 6 months. On entering the CA-4 bloc (Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua) they get **one shared 90-day stay**, and crossing between CA-4 countries does **not** restart it. Guatemala's IGM can extend that stay **once, by up to 90 more days** (US$25, at IGM in Guatemala City), for a maximum of about 180 days as a tourist.

### Cited Findings
**Visa status**
- Guatemala removed the visa requirement for Colombians in 2013, by Acuerdo Ministerial 322-2013 — [Cancillería de Colombia, "Con Guatemala, ya son 32 los países que no exigen visa a los colombianos" [official], 2013](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/sala-de-prensa/noticias/con-guatemala-ya-son-32-los-paises-que-no-exigen-visa-los-colombianos)
- Guatemala now sorts nationalities into visa categories under **Acuerdo IGM-015-2025 (Reglamento de Visas Guatemaltecas)**. "Categoría A" (visa-exempt) travellers enter with a valid passport only. Category B must apply at a consulate — [livinginguatemala.com, "Visa Guatemala por nacionalidad", 2026 (blog)](https://livinginguatemala.com/es/visas-a-guatemala/visa-guatemala-por-nacionalidad/). An official 2025–26 IGM list naming Colombia in Category A was not retrieved.
- El Salvador is marked "NO" (does not require a visa from Colombians) in the Colombian foreign ministry's country table dated **May 2026**. The table warns that the information can change at any time — [Cancillería de Colombia, "Lista Países exigen visa mayo 2026" (PDF) [official], May 2026](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/sites/default/files/FOTOS2025/Lista-Paises-exigen-visa-mayo-2026.pdf)
- A 2025 report says El Salvador's DGME visa list includes only two Latin American nations, Bolivia and Venezuela — [elsalvador.com, "¿Quiénes necesitan visa para entrar a El Salvador?", 2025](https://www.elsalvador.com/turismo/cultura-viva/paises-visas-para-entrar-a-el-salvador/1250791/2025/)
- A Colombian TV news report (Feb 2026) says Colombians can enter Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Panamá and Belize without a visa for short trips. It adds that officers may ask for an exit ticket, proof of accommodation, an invitation letter or proof of funds — [Noticias RCN, "Países centroamericanos que aceptan colombianos sin visa", Feb 2026](https://www.noticiasrcn.com/economia/paises-centroamericanos-sin-visa-colombianos-solo-pasaporte-993903). **Caution:** I could not verify RCN's inclusion of Nicaragua (see Gaps).
- Honduras requires a passport valid for at least 6 months — [El Heraldo (Honduras), "Ingreso a Honduras: guía completa de requisitos", date not shown](https://www.elheraldo.hn/honduras/ingreso-honduras-guia-completa-requisitos-documentos-viaje-turistas-IB18919188)
- For Guatemala, Assist Card says the passport must be valid for at least 6 months from the date of entry — [Assist Card Colombia, "Requisitos para viajar a Guatemala", date not shown](https://www.assistcard.com/co/requisitos-viajar-guatemala)
- A 2021 article from Guatemala's state news agency says Category A entrants may still be asked for proof of funds, a hotel booking and a return ticket — [AGN Guatemala, 2021 (possibly outdated)](https://agn.gt/?p=97351)

**CA-4 shared stay and extensions**
- Guatemala's Código de Migración, art. 74: tourists who entered regularly may stay "no más de 90 días, prorrogable una vez" (up to 90 days, extendable once) — [IGM, "Atención extranjeros, ¿Sabe cómo ampliar su estadía en Guatemala?" [official], date not shown](https://igm.gob.gt/atencion-extranjeros-sabe-como-ampliar-su-estadia-en-guatemala/)
- IGM extension (prórroga) requirements:
  - the extension request form
  - a valid passport with the last Guatemala entry stamp
  - proof of solvency: a travel ticket, an international credit card, or a letter from a Guatemalan guarantor
  - a **US$25** payment receipt
  - if the entry stamp is missing, a Certificación de Movimiento Migratorio (**+US$10**)

  The request must be filed **before** the authorized stay expires; otherwise a fine for irregular stay applies — [IGM [official]](https://igm.gob.gt/atencion-extranjeros-sabe-como-ampliar-su-estadia-en-guatemala/)
- If a traveller needs more time once the extension is used up, IGM says they must apply for temporary residence — [IGM [official]](https://igm.gob.gt/atencion-extranjeros-sabe-como-ampliar-su-estadia-en-guatemala/)
- IGM's unified CA-4 procedures manual — [IGM, "MANUAL-CA4-Unificado-2.pdf" [official], uploaded April 2022](https://igm.gob.gt/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/MANUAL-CA4-Unificado-2.pdf) (content not retrievable in detail)
- The 90 days cover the four CA-4 countries together, not each country. Crossing between them does not reset the count. The extension adds up to 90 days, for a maximum of 180 days as a tourist — [livinginguatemala.com, "Guatemala Visa 2026: 90 Days Free + How to Stay Longer", 2026 (blog)](https://livinginguatemala.com/blog/guatemala-visa-guide/); [livinginguatemala.com, "The IGM Prórroga Checklist… (2026)" (blog)](https://livinginguatemala.com/visas-to-guatemala/igm-prorroga-checklist)
- A source says being expelled from one CA-4 country means exclusion from all of them — [livinginguatemala.com, "Guía visa Guatemala" (blog), 2026](https://livinginguatemala.com/es/blog/guia-visa-guatemala/)
- **Conflict on resetting the 90 days:** one third-party guide says that after 180 days (90 + 90) you must leave the CA-4 region for at least 72 hours before coming back — [livinginguatemala.com, "CA-4 Border Runs & Resetting Your Guatemala Tourist Visa (2026)" (blog)](https://livinginguatemala.com/visas-to-guatemala/ca-4-border-run-guatemala/). Another third-party page says IGM publishes no minimum time outside, and re-entry is up to the border officer. I found the 72-hour rule in no official source.
- **Conflict on the current form:** third-party pages give the prórroga form as a January 2026 revision or as "PVT-IGM-01-2026" — [livinginguatemala.com prórroga checklist (blog)](https://livinginguatemala.com/visas-to-guatemala/igm-prorroga-checklist). Not confirmed officially.
- Under the CA-4 agreement, a visa issued by one member is honoured by all four, and the time limit covers total time across the zone. The arrangement itself is unchanged in 2026 — [Infobae, "Qué países del mundo aún pueden ingresar al CA-4 sin visa tras la reforma migratoria…", 14 Aug 2026](https://www.infobae.com/nicaragua/2026/08/14/que-paises-del-mundo-aun-pueden-ingresar-al-ca-4-sin-visa-tras-la-reforma-migratoria-mas-profunda-que-ha-vivido-centroamerica-en-decadas/); [Wikipedia, "Central America-4 Border Control Agreement"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_America-4_Border_Control_Agreement)
- **2026 change in the CA-4 bloc (Nicaragua only):** Nicaragua published a migration reform in its official gazette on 28 Feb 2026 (Wikipedia gives 16 Feb 2026 as the effective date). It ended visa-free entry for citizens of 128 countries, including the US, Canada, the UK, the EU, Mexico, Brazil and Argentina. Those travellers now need a Category C "visa consultada" authorised from Managua, which takes 30 days or more and costs about US$30–50 — [Infobae, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.infobae.com/nicaragua/2026/08/14/que-paises-del-mundo-aun-pueden-ingresar-al-ca-4-sin-visa-tras-la-reforma-migratoria-mas-profunda-que-ha-vivido-centroamerica-en-decadas/); [Wikipedia, "Visa policy of Nicaragua"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_policy_of_Nicaragua). I found **no** CA-4-wide reform affecting Guatemala, El Salvador or Honduras.

### Inferences
- The CA-4 clock starts at the **first** CA-4 entry. If the couple rides in from Nicaragua, the count starts there, not at the Honduran border. Riding Honduras, El Salvador and Guatemala at touring pace (often 4–8 weeks) normally fits inside 90 days. If they are slower, the cleanest fix is a prórroga at IGM in Guatemala City (US$25), filed before day 90.
- Leaving the CA-4 for Mexico or Belize ends the CA-4 stay. Because they continue north into Mexico, they will not need a reset.
- Officers can still ask visa-exempt Colombians for funds, lodging or onward-travel evidence. Carrying a printed summary of the route, bank statements, and an onward plan (for example a booked flight from Mexico City to Colombia) is prudent in all three countries.

### Gaps
- I found no official 2025–2026 Honduran (INM Honduras) or Salvadoran (DGME) page listing Colombians as visa-exempt. The Cancillería May 2026 table snippet confirmed El Salvador only; Honduras and Guatemala rows were not visible in the retrieved extract.
- Whether **Nicaragua** now requires a visa from Colombians after the Feb 2026 reform is **unverified**. The search quota ran out before I could check. RCN (Feb 2026) lists Nicaragua as visa-free, but this predates or ignores the reform and should not be relied on. This matters for the CA-4 entry point and is likely covered by the Costa Rica–Nicaragua researcher.
- I found no source on how or where to extend a CA-4 stay in Honduras or El Salvador, only in Guatemala.

## 2. Land-border entry and exit fees, and the main crossings cyclists use (El Amatillo, El Poy, Agua Caliente, Las Chinamas, Pedro de Alvarado, La Hachadura)

### Takeaway
- **Honduras:** charges a small cash entry fee at land borders, consistently reported as about **US$3**. Some travellers report up to US$5, and touts at El Amatillo pass off fake extra fees, so ask for a receipt.
- **El Salvador:** no 2025–2026 source shows a land-entry fee. Whether Colombians pay the US$12 "tarjeta de turismo" is unresolved.
- **Guatemala:** has no official exit fee by land.
- **Crossings:** heading north, the main 24-hour crossings are El Amatillo or El Poy (Honduras→El Salvador), then Las Chinamas–Valle Nuevo or La Hachadura–Pedro de Alvarado (El Salvador→Guatemala). Most traveller reports describe these crossings in the opposite direction, El Salvador→Honduras.

### Cited Findings
**Honduras**
- Honduras's migration regulation lists a **US$3.00** fee for non-Central-American foreigners entering by land — [Reglamento de la Ley de Migración y Extranjería, published 3 May 2004 [official, old]](https://www.tsc.gob.hn/web/leyes/Reglamento%20de%20la%20Ley%20Migraci%C3%B3n%20(07).pdf)
- Spain's foreign ministry says foreigners with ordinary passports entering Honduras by land must pay a "tasa por servicios migratorios" in cash, and advises asking for a receipt — [Ministerio de Asuntos Exteriores de España, "Recomendaciones de viaje: Honduras", date not shown](https://www.exteriores.gob.es/es/ServiciosAlCiudadano/Paginas/Detalle-recomendaciones-de-viaje.aspx?trc=Honduras)
- Traveller guides put the land entry fee at US$3, paid inside the office just before the stamp, and requested in US dollars. Money changers are usually around — [Mochilero sin dinero, Nicaragua–Honduras crossing guide, date not shown](https://mochilerosindinero.com/america/nicaragua/621-como-cruzar-la-frontera-de-nicaragua-a-honduras-tasas-y-tramites)
- El Amatillo and El Poy are both open 24 hours and both charge the US$3 Honduran processing fee — [Bookaway blog, date not shown](https://www.bookaway.com/blog/border-crossing-el-salvador-honduras/); [Road Affair, "Central America: Border Crossing Fees", date not shown](https://www.roadaffair.com/border-crossing-fees-central-america/)
- One traveller reported paying US$5 per person — [couldhavestayedhome.com, El Salvador–Honduras overland, date not shown](https://couldhavestayedhome.com/el-salvador/el-salvador-to-honduras-overland-border-crossing/)
- Scams such as fake fees are "rampant" at El Amatillo. Some travellers report US$3 (75 HNL) "exit charges"; demand a receipt — [bordercrossinghub.com, El Amatillo, date not shown](https://bordercrossinghub.com/el-amatillo-la-union-border-crossing/); [trans-americas.com, date not shown](https://trans-americas.com/border-crossing-honduras-to-el-salvador/)
- One motorcyclist at El Poy did not remember paying any fee to enter Honduras. This is anecdotal and conflicts with the US$3 reports — [terraxplor.com, El Poy by motorcycle, date not shown](https://www.terraxplor.com/post/el-salvador-honduras-el-poy)
- A 2011 decree (252-2011) created a **US$17** security fee on nationals and foreigners entering or leaving by air, sea and land — [El Heraldo, "Gobierno impone tasa de 17 dólares al entrar o salir de Honduras", 2011](https://www.elheraldo.hn/economia/gobierno-impone-tasa-de-17-dolares-al-entrar-o-salir-de-honduras-ILEH608451). **Outdated or unclear:** no recent source shows it being charged at land crossings.
- Honduras's online "prechequeo" (prechequeo.inm.gob.hn) **stopped being mandatory** for entering or leaving Honduras, according to an INM announcement of 12 Dec 2023. It stays mandatory only for Nicaraguans, seven days before travel — [El Universal (Colombia), "Honduras elimina prechequeo migratorio excepto para nicaragüenses", Dec 2023](https://www.eluniversal.com.co/palante-chamos-y-chamas/honduras-elimina-prechequeo-migratorio-excepto-para-nicaraguenses-MG9998349); [Tunota, 14 Feb 2024](https://www.tunota.com/mundo/pre-chequeo-migracion-honduras-requisito-2024-02-14). Some commercial guides still call it mandatory, which is probably out of date.
- Reports describe irregular charges on migrants in transit through Honduras, including transport overcharging near Las Manos. These do not concern the standard tourist entry fee — [Resumen Latinoamericano, Oct 2022](https://www.resumenlatinoamericano.org/2022/10/25/migrantes-siguen-altos-e-irregulares-cobros-de-tasas-a-personas-en-transito-por-honduras/); [El Heraldo](https://www.elheraldo.hn/honduras/transportistas-cobran-alto-costo-migrantes-zona-oriental-BM14810133)

**El Salvador**
- El Salvador's official tourism site says some visa-exempt nationalities may have to buy a **US$12 tourist card** at the border — [elsalvador.travel, "About El Salvador" [official tourism], date not shown](https://elsalvador.travel/about-el-salvador/). A 2026 guide repeats about US$12 for up to 90 days — [elsalvador.com, "¿Cuánto cuesta viajar a El Salvador en 2026?", 2026](https://www.elsalvador.com/turismo/rutas-y-aventuras/cuanto-cuesta-viajar-a-el-salvador-en-2026/1276196/2026/)
- **Conflict:** another travel site states "No Visa, No Tourist Card" for El Salvador — [onmetravel.com, date not shown](https://onmetravel.com/el-salvador-entry-requirements). RCN (Feb 2026) says Colombians must buy a tourist card of about US$12 — [Noticias RCN](https://www.noticiasrcn.com/economia/paises-centroamericanos-sin-visa-colombianos-solo-pasaporte-993903). El Salvador's Migration Law text mentions US$10, which is likely outdated — [Ley de Migración (eregulations)](https://elsalvador.eregulations.org/media/LEY%20DE%20MIGRACI%C3%93N.PDF)
- Las Chinamas handles about 95% of the national and foreign tourists entering El Salvador or leaving for Guatemala. The post has been fully renovated — [Ministerio de Seguridad Pública y Justicia (SV) [official], date not shown](https://www.seguridad.gob.sv/paso-fronterizo-las-chinamas-totalmente-renovado/); [La Página, more than 500,000 entries via Las Chinamas](https://lapagina.com.sv/nacionales/las-chinamas-destaca-como-punto-clave-para-el-turismo-terrestre-tras-mas-de-500-mil-ingresos-a-el-salvador/)
- The PNC says Las Chinamas and La Hachadura operate 24 hours for national and foreign tourists — [PNC El Salvador on X [official], 2026](https://x.com/PNCSV/status/2109378293707186457)
- The four El Salvador–Guatemala crossings are Valle Nuevo–Las Chinamas, San Cristóbal–Anguiatú, El Jobo–San Cristóbal and La Hachadura–Pedro de Alvarado. They have a CA-4 fast lane for CA-4 citizens; foreigners use the normal booths — [Infobae, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.infobae.com/nicaragua/2026/08/14/que-paises-del-mundo-aun-pueden-ingresar-al-ca-4-sin-visa-tras-la-reforma-migratoria-mas-profunda-que-ha-vivido-centroamerica-en-decadas/)
- There is no fee to leave El Salvador at El Poy — [theblogofdimi.com, El Salvador to Honduras via El Poy, date not shown](https://theblogofdimi.com/el-salvador-honduras-bus-el-poy-border/)

**Guatemala**
- Leaving Guatemala by land carries **no exit fee**, and Guatemala's migration tariff has no exit-fee line — [livinginguatemala.com, "Border run costs 2026" (blog, cites the IGM tariff)](https://livinginguatemala.com/visas-to-guatemala/border-run-costs-2026/)

### Inferences
- Budget about US$3–5 per person in small US-dollar bills for the Honduran entry. Pay only inside the immigration office and against a receipt. Expect no official charge to leave El Salvador or Guatemala. Have US$12 each ready in case El Salvador applies the tourist card.
- Within the CA-4, the counters process foreigners separately from the CA-4 citizen fast lanes, so expect to walk the bikes into the immigration hall at each crossing.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 primary source for the current Honduran land fee amount. The US$3 figure comes from a 2004 regulation and traveller reports.
- Whether El Salvador charges Colombians the US$12 tourist card is **unresolved**: sources conflict, and none is a current DGME list.
- I found no sources on **Agua Caliente** (Honduras–Guatemala), Corinto or El Florido, or on Pedro de Alvarado specifically beyond its listing.
- I found nothing on bicycle-specific rules or customs forms at CA-4 land borders.

## 3. Belize (if they detour): visa, stay and fees

### Takeaway
Colombians have been visa-exempt in Belize since December 2014, and the Colombian foreign ministry's May 2026 table still marks Belize "NO visa". Stay length is reported as 30 days (most travel sources) or up to 90 days (a few). Leaving Belize overland costs **BZ$40 (about US$20) per person, in cash**.

### Cited Findings
- Belize removed the visa requirement for Colombians. The Cancillería notice says the waiver applies to arrivals at Philip Goldson International Airport or Belize's seaports — [Cancillería de Colombia, "35 países no piden visa a los colombianos; Belice se suma a esta lista" [official], 2014](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/newsroom/news/35-paises-no-piden-visa-colombianos-belice-suma-lista). Wikipedia dates the change to Dec 2014 — [Wikipedia, "Relaciones entre Belice y Colombia"](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relaciones_entre_Belice_y_Colombia)
- The Cancillería May 2026 country table marks Belize "NO" (no visa required of Colombians) — [Cancillería, Lista Países exigen visa, May 2026 [official]](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/sites/default/files/FOTOS2025/Lista-Paises-exigen-visa-mayo-2026.pdf)
- Sherpa says Colombian passport holders do not need a visa for Belize — [Sherpa, date not shown](https://apply.joinsherpa.com/visa/belize/colombian-citizens?language=en-US)
- **Stay-length conflict:** 30 days, from Tropic Air, which lists Colombia among nationals needing no visa for stays under 30 days — [Tropic Air, "Belize Immigration & Visas", about 2026](https://www.tropicair.com/belize-immigration-visas/); BCD Travel 2018 also gives 30 days — [BCD Travel Colombia PDF, 2018 (outdated)](https://documentacionembajadas.bcdtravel.com.co/docs/documentacion/CONDICIONES_DE_VIAJE_PAX_CENTROAMERICA_2018.pdf). Against that, 90 days — [visarequirements.info, 2026](https://visarequirements.info/countries/colombia/belize/) and Wikipedia.
- Tropic Air points to the Belize Department of Immigration page (immigration.gov.bz/visa/visa-who-qualify/) as the authoritative list — [Tropic Air](https://www.tropicair.com/belize-immigration-visas/)
- Visit World says visitors need a return ticket and enough money, about **US$75 per day** — [visitworld.today, date not shown (secondary)](https://visitworld.today/belize/colombia-citizenship/travel)
- **Land exit fee:** departure fee BZ$30.00, plus border development fee BZ$2.50, plus PACT conservation fee BZ$7.50, for a total of **BZ$40.00 (about US$20)**, cash. This is from Border Management Agency fee pages checked on 2026-09-16, and it applies to non-citizens and non-permanent residents — [livinginguatemala.com, "Melchor de Mencos border crossing" (blog citing BMA)](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/melchor-de-mencos-border-crossing/); [livinginguatemala.com, "Flores to San Ignacio"](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/flores-to-san-ignacio-belize/)
- Belize waived exit fees at Santa Elena (the Mexican border) for visitors from five Mexican states staying 7 days or less, approved by Cabinet in Feb 2024. This does not apply to Colombians, and I found nothing confirming it is still in force — [Amandala, 2024](https://amandala.com.bz/news/belize-to-waive-border-exit-taxes-from-selected-mexican-state-visitors)
- An April 2026 proposal would raise the **air** departure fee by BZ$32.50, not the land fee. It was still a proposal when reported — [San Pedro Sun, 14 Apr 2026](https://www.sanpedrosun.com/?p=57235)
- A 2018 agency document lists a yellow-fever vaccination certificate, applied 10 days before travel, for Belize and other Central American countries — [BCD Travel Colombia, 2018 (outdated; verify)](https://documentacionembajadas.bcdtravel.com.co/docs/documentacion/CONDICIONES_DE_VIAJE_PAX_CENTROAMERICA_2018.pdf)

### Inferences
- A Belize detour (Guatemala via Melchor de Mencos/Benque Viejo → Belize → Santa Elena/Chetumal, Mexico) is visa-free but adds US$20 per person to leave Belize. It also means entering Mexico at Chetumal (Quintana Roo) instead of Chiapas, which avoids the higher-risk Chiapas border zone (see section 6).
- Ask for the days you need at Belize entry, since 30 days appears to be the default stamp.

### Gaps
- The 2014 waiver notice mentions air and sea arrivals. I could not confirm in an official Belize source that **land** arrivals by Colombians are covered, although travel sources imply they are. Check immigration.gov.bz or the Belize embassy before the detour.
- No official 2026 confirmation of the 30- versus 90-day stay.
- No source on Belize land entry fees, as opposed to exit fees.
- Not established whether holding a US visa changes anything for Colombians in Belize.

## 4. Mexico: do Colombians need a visa or electronic authorization in 2026, and what changed in 2024–2026?

### Takeaway
**No.** As of the latest sources (May 2026), Colombians remain **visa-exempt** in Mexico as tourists for up to 180 days. Mexico has **not** reimposed a visa on Colombians, unlike Ecuador (2021), Venezuela and Brazil (2021–22) and Peru (2024). Colombians are **not** part of the SAE electronic authorization, which covers only Russia, Ukraine and Turkey (air travel). What Colombians do have is a mandatory, free **online "pre-registro"** (INM, since 1 April 2022) that the official texts describe as applying to **air** arrivals. I found no source that applies it to land entries.

### Cited Findings
- Colombian nationals do not need a visa to enter Mexico as "Visitantes sin permiso para realizar actividades remuneradas" (visitors not permitted to do paid work) — [Consulado de Colombia en México, "Los nacionales colombianos no requieren visa para ingresar a México" [official], 2022](https://mexico.consulado.gov.co/sala-de-prensa/noticias/los-nacionales-colombianos-no-requieren-visa-para-ingresar-mexico); [Embajada de México en Colombia [official], date not shown](https://embamex.sre.gob.mx/colombia/index.php/nueva)
- 2026 guides confirm: no visa for tourism, business or transit under 180 days; passport valid for at least 6 months recommended — [Holafly, "Requisitos para viajar a México desde Colombia (2026)"](https://esim.holafly.com/es/blog/planificar-viaje/requisitos-viajar-mexico-desde-colombia/); [Assist Card Colombia, "Requisitos para viajar a México desde Colombia en 2026"](https://www.assistcard.com/co/requisitos-viajar-mexico-colombia)
- **Pre-registro (since 1 Apr 2022):** a joint SRE–INM statement says the electronic pre-registration system applies to Colombian nationals **arriving by air**. Completing it generates a QR code and the Forma Migratoria Múltiple (FMM), both to be printed and shown to INM officers on arrival — [INM/gob.mx, "Inicia aplicación del sistema de pre registro electrónico entre México y Colombia" [official], April 2022](https://www.gob.mx/inm/prensa/inicia-aplicacion-del-sistema-de-pre-registro-electronico-entre-mexico-y-colombia); [SRE version [official]](https://www.gob.mx/sre/prensa/inicia-la-aplicacion-del-sistema-de-pre-registro-electronico-entre-mexico-y-colombia?idiom=es)
- Colombia's foreign ministry calls the pre-registro mandatory, with the QR and FMM to be shown at Mexico's international airports — [Cancillería de Colombia, "Antes de viajar a México, diligencie el Pre registro" [official], 2022](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/sala-de-prensa/noticias/antes-de-viajar-mexico-diligencie-el-pre-registro)
- The INM platform user manual says the pre-registro applies to Colombians regardless of country of residence.   - Brazil's exact date in the Wikipedia table (shown as 18 Aug 2022) was flagged as unclear by the search summary and is not verified here

### Inferences
- For a land entry from Guatemala, the official texts do not require the pre-registro, because it was designed around airline arrivals. The land process is the FMM at the border (section 5). Completing it if the platform accepts land arrivals would do no harm, but this is untested (see Gaps).
- Nothing in 2024–2026 reporting points to an imminent visa for Colombians. Still, Mexico's pattern (Ecuador, Brazil, Venezuela, Peru) has been to reimpose visas quickly, with a few weeks' notice, after spikes in inadmissions or irregular transit. Re-check gob.mx/inm and the Cancillería within 2 weeks of reaching the Mexican border.
- A US visa is **not** needed by Colombians for Mexico, since they are already visa-exempt. Mexico's waiver for valid US visa holders matters for visa-required nationalities. Whether a US visa helps a Colombian at the INM interview is not documented.

### Gaps
- No official statement on whether the pre-registro applies to, or can be completed for, **land** arrivals.
- The Mexico row of the Cancillería May 2026 table was not visible in the retrieved extract.
- The exact Brazil reimposition date was not verified.

## 5. Mexico at a land border: what INM officers ask Colombians, the FMM process, the DNR fee and its 2026 amount, maximum stay, and refusal rates

### Takeaway
At a southern land crossing:
1. Get stamped out of Guatemala.
2. Walk the bikes across.
3. Get an **FMM** and passport stamp from INM; the officer writes the number of days, **up to 180** at their discretion.
4. Clear customs.

For land entries of more than 7 days you must pay the **DNR (Derecho de No Residente): MXN 983 in 2026** per person. Pay at a Banjercito module, a bank with the INM "hoja de ayuda", or online. A 2027 increase to about MXN 1,158 is advancing in Congress. Colombians face Mexico's highest inadmission numbers (about **53,449 refused in 2024**, roughly 8% of arrivals), almost all of it reported at **airports**. Officers ask about purpose, funds, lodging and return or onward travel.

### Cited Findings
**What officers ask (documented for air arrivals; the same INM criteria apply at land posts)**
- Colombia's foreign ministry: tourists will be asked to prove economic solvency (bank statements, credit cards, work letter) and a hotel booking, preferably paid, or an invitation letter if staying with family or friends. A round-trip ticket consistent with the booking dates is important. Answer the officer clearly and honestly. Meeting the requirements does not guarantee entry. If refused, you may contact the consulate and a relative — [Cancillería de Colombia, "Advertencia a los viajeros: viaje informado a México" [official], date not shown](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/sala-de-prensa/noticias/advertencia-los-viajeros-viaje-informado-mexico); [VivaAerobus, "Guía del viajero colombiano en México" (PDF), date not shown](https://content.vivaaerobus.com/pwa/guia-viajero-colombiano-en-mexico.pdf)
- A daily budget estimate of about **US$100–500 per day** appeared in the summary of these guidance documents. I could not tell whether it comes from the Cancillería page or the VivaAerobus guide — [Cancillería](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/sala-de-prensa/noticias/advertencia-los-viajeros-viaje-informado-mexico); [VivaAerobus guide](https://content.vivaaerobus.com/pwa/guia-viajero-colombiano-en-mexico.pdf)

**FMM process at land borders**
- Land arrivals need the FMM, processed at the point of entry — [touristcardmx.com, "Requisitos de Entrada a México para Colombianos en 2026" (commercial site)](https://www.touristcardmx.com/es/requisitos/colombianos)
- At Guatemala–Mexico crossings the process is: exit at Guatemalan migration, cross the bridge, get stamped at Mexican migration. The FMM is filled on paper at the border — [Holafly, "Requisitos para viajar a México desde Guatemala (2026)"](https://esim.holafly.com/es/blog/planificar-viaje/requisitos-viajar-guatemala-mexico/). Holafly's fee figure of about MXN 638 is **outdated**; it is below the 2024 DNR of MXN 717 and the 2026 DNR of MXN 983.
- Keep the FMM intact: a traveller with a torn FMM was charged MXN 500 on exit — [bucketlistbri.com, San Cristóbal–Panajachel crossing, Nov 2025](https://bucketlistbri.com/san-cristobal-to-panajachel/)
- In some cases INM agents stamp the passport and handwrite the number of days instead of issuing a paper FMM — [Guadalajara Reporter, "INM switches FMM forms for passport stamps", older](https://theguadalajarareporter.net/index.php/news/news/national/58555-inm-switches-fmm-forms-for-passport-stamps)
- At Ciudad Hidalgo (Oct 2025, on a motorcycle) immigration plus Banjercito took about 5 minutes, and a customs officer inspected the bike — [terraxplor.com, "Hidalgo Mexico to Guatemala border crossing by motorcycle", Oct 2025](https://www.terraxplor.com/post/mexico-hidalgo-guatemala)

**DNR (Derecho de No Residente)**
- The 2026 DNR for visitors is **MXN 983**, set in the Ley Federal de Derechos and effective 1 Jan 2026 — [Clark Hill, "Mexico increases government fees for immigration procedures effective January 1, 2026"](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/mexico-increases-government-fees-for-immigration-procedures-effective-january-1-2026). A related DOF publication of 8 Jan 2026 — [DOF, 8 Jan 2026 [official]](https://dof.gob.mx/2026/SHCP/SHCP_080126.pdf). Search summaries describe the 2026 rate as a 14.2% rise over 2025.
- **Land rule:** if you enter by land and stay **less than 7 days**, no DNR. Beyond 7 days the DNR is charged; the guide indicates it is collected **when leaving the country**. The 2026 guide gives **MXN 983**; the 2024 guide gave MXN 717. The DNR can be paid:
  - at any bank branch on the northern or southern border, using the INM "Hoja de ayuda", then showing the bank receipt to INM
  - at Banjercito modules, with no hoja de ayuda needed
  - online at https://www.inm.gob.mx/fmme/publico/solicitud.html

  Banjercito does not work through agents, social media or individuals outside its facilities — [Gobierno de México, "Guía completa Héroes Paisanos" (PDF) [official], 2025–26](https://www.gob.mx/cms/uploads/attachment/file/1082677/GUIA_COMPLETA_HHP_ESP_2.pdf); [ANAM, Guía Héroes Paisanos 2025 [official]](https://www.anam.gob.mx/wp-content/uploads/guia_espanol_heroes_paisanos_2025.pdf); [ANAM, Guía Paisano 2024 [official]](https://www.anam.gob.mx/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/GUIA_PAISANO_2024.pdf)
- **2027 change in progress:** the original 2027 economic package proposed +35.8%, to about MXN 1,334.80 — [El Tiempo (Monclova), Sept 2026](https://www.tiempo.com.mx/economia/piden-hoteleros-reconsiderar-subidon-35-por-ciento-dnr-septiembre-2026/); [Stamped Nomad](https://www.stampednomad.com/digital-nomad-news/mexico-proposes-raising-foreign-visitor-entry-fee-to-1-334-pesos-cj65a1). The Chamber of Deputies' Hacienda committee then endorsed **MXN 1,158** (+17.8%) for 2027, still pending final approval as of 13 Oct 2026 — [Crónica, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.cronica.com.mx/nacional/2026/10/13/proponen-cuota-de-mil-158-pesos-para-el-dnr-en-2027-buscan-fortalecer-infraestructura-migratoria/). Business groups in Baja California Sur oppose it — [Zeta Tijuana, Oct 2026](https://zetatijuana.com/2026/10/empresarios-de-bcs-van-contra-aumento-al-dnr/)

**Maximum stay and officer discretion**
- INM's 2023 traveller guide: on air or sea arrival the INM stamp authorises a **maximum** of 180 days — [INM/gob.mx, "2023 Guía del Viajero – Visa" (PDF) [official], 2023](https://www.gob.mx/cms/uploads/attachment/file/793746/2023_GUIA_DEL_VIAJERO_Visa.pdf)
- "Maximum 180 days" no longer means 180 by default. The agent writes the number of days, and growing numbers of visitors get 30 days or fewer. Many local INM offices no longer extend FMMs — [Mexico News Daily, about 2025](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/news/a-mexican-visitors-permit-no-longer-guarantees-a-180-day-stay/); [Stamped Nomad, "Mexico officers cut FMM stays below 180 days"](https://www.stampednomad.com/updates/mexico-officers-cut-fmm-stays-below-180-days-for-nomads-and-tourists)

**Refusals and inadmission of Colombians**
- In **2024**, 53,449 of 666,637 Colombians arriving at Mexican airports were refused (about 8%), per Mexico's Unidad de Política Migratoria. Colombia had the highest number of inadmitted nationals of any country — [Infobae Colombia, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2025/03/26/mas-de-53000-colombianos-fueron-inadmitidos-en-mexico-evite-ser-uno-de-ellos-con-estas-recomendaciones/); [La Jornada, "Colombianos, los viajeros más rechazados al arribar a México", 24 Mar 2025](https://www.jornada.com.mx/2025/03/24/politica/009n2pol); [El País (Cali)](https://www.elpais.com.co/colombia/crecen-las-inadmisiones-de-colombianos-en-mexico-mas-de-50000-personas-han-sufrido-las-consecuencias-2351.html)
- 51,108 Colombians were inadmitted in a year (to Jan 2025) at the three main destinations — [Semana, 2025](https://www.semana.com/politica/articulo/inadmitidos-en-mexico-51108-colombianos-fueron-deportados-en-un-ano-estos-son-sus-dramaticos-relatos/202536/). More than 38,000 were refused at Cancún and Mexico City airports — [24 Horas Quintana Roo, 20 Feb 2025](https://24horasqroo.mx/2025/02/20/cancun-cdmx-aeropuertos/)
- Colombia's ambassador said that of about 106,000 Colombian travellers, about 8,120 were refused, close to 1 in 10. The main causes cited were missing requirements or badly completed forms — [Infobae, 2 Jul 2024](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2024/07/02/prohibiciones-a-turistas-colombianos-en-mexico-preocupan-a-autoridades-colombianas/); [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/revista-portafolio/por-que-visitar-mexico-se-ha-convertido-en-una-odisea-par-los-turistas-colombianos-607852)
- A Colombian doctor filed a mistreatment complaint against INM — [Proceso, 19 Mar 2025](https://www.proceso.com.mx/nacional/2025/3/19/no-parare-hasta-que-se-haga-justicia-medico-colombiano-denuncia-al-inm-por-maltrato-346039.html)
- An old forum comment says a prior refusal at the Mexico City airport may stay on record and complicate later entries (anecdotal) — [elcaminomascorto.com, La Mesilla–Ciudad Cuauhtémoc thread, old](https://elcaminomascorto.com/frontera-mexico-ciudad-de-cuauhtemoc-guatemala-la-mesilla-requisitos/)

### Inferences
- Budget **MXN 983 per person** for the DNR if entering in 2026, or possibly **MXN 1,158** if entering from 1 Jan 2027 and the increase passes. A trip from Chiapas to north of Mexico City will take well over 7 days, so the DNR will apply.
- Cyclists can't show a typical hotel-and-return-flight package, so prepare a land-traveller version:
  - a printed route and itinerary (Chiapas → Oaxaca/Puebla → CDMX → end point), with rough dates
  - a **booked or refundable flight home from Mexico City** (or the end city) to Colombia, inside the days requested
  - bank statements or credit cards for both riders; at the commonly cited US$100 per day, a 60-day trip implies showing on the order of US$6,000 each, which is the conservative end
  - the first few nights' accommodation booked
  - proof of ties in Colombia (work letter, studies, property)
- Ask explicitly for the number of days needed, with a margin of at least 30 extra. Extensions are hard to get.
- The published inadmission statistics are airport data. I found no data on Colombians refused at southern land posts, so the land risk can't be quantified.

### Gaps
- No reports specific to **Colombians entering Mexico by land** from Guatemala in 2024–2026, and no land-border refusal statistics.
- No complete 2025 or 2026 inadmission totals.
- Whether INM at southern land posts now issues the digital FMM or paper forms, and whether DNR is collected at entry or exit at each specific post, was not confirmed for Ciudad Hidalgo, Talismán, Ciudad Cuauhtémoc or El Ceibo.

## 6. Guatemala–Mexico crossings for cyclists (Tecún Umán–Ciudad Hidalgo, El Carmen–Talismán, La Mesilla–Ciudad Cuauhtémoc, El Ceibo–Tenosique): INM service and security

### Takeaway
All four are formal crossings with Mexican immigration (INM), customs and Banjercito. Recent traveller reports (late 2025) describe **El Carmen–Talismán** as quiet and the easiest, and **Ciudad Hidalgo** as fast and fully staffed. **La Mesilla** is busier and sits inside UK and US government warning zones for border violence, with a June 2025 shoot-out. **El Ceibo–Tenosique** (Petén → Tabasco) is the lower-risk state at US advisory level (Tabasco Level 2 versus Chiapas Level 3). It runs along a migrant corridor with many checkpoints and long empty stretches.

### Cited Findings
**INM and services**
- Ciudad Hidalgo (officially referred to as Puerto Fronterizo Schiate II in the report): in Oct 2025 a border official told a motorcyclist that Talismán was the wrong choice for vehicles and that Hidalgo was the right one. Immigration plus Banjercito took about 5 minutes, and customs inspected the vehicle — [terraxplor.com, Oct 2025](https://www.terraxplor.com/post/mexico-hidalgo-guatemala). This was a motor-vehicle import, which bicycles don't need.
- El Carmen–Talismán: quiet, no queues, described as the most convenient option compared with the busier Tecún Umán and La Mesilla (Dec 2025, Mexico→Guatemala direction) — [theblogofdimi.com, "Crossing the El Carmen–Talisman Border", Dec 2025](https://theblogofdimi.com/el-carmen-talisman-border-mexico-guatemala/)
- La Mesilla: a border town with little reason to stay; money changers give poor rates — [CycleBlaze, "bikebimbleii" journal, Panajachel to La Mesilla](https://www.cycleblaze.com/journals/bikebimbleii/day-150-152-panajachel-la-mesilla-border-town/). Guatemalan customs is at km 336 of the CA-1, Aldea La Mesilla; the Mexican side is Ciudad Cuauhtémoc, Frontera Comalapa municipality, Chiapas — [livinginguatemala.com, "La Mesilla Border Crossing… [2026]" (blog)](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/la-mesilla-border-crossing)
- An older cycling guide says bicycles can cross the Usumacinta by small boat at Bethel (Guatemala) to Frontera Corozal (Chiapas), which vehicles can't do. Current status unverified — [tour.tk, "Mexico country info" (old)](http://www.tour.tk/country-information/mexico.htm)
- Overview of several crossings — [Adam Isacson, "Some Mexico–Guatemala Border Crossings"](https://adamisacson.com/some-mexico-guatemala-border-crossings/)
- In the past, Mexican authorities restricted informal crossing over the Suchiate river and required use of the bridge with documents — [INM, "Tema Migratorio 220321" [official], 2021](https://www.inm.gob.mx/gobmx/word/index.php/tema-migratorio-220321/)

**Security**
- The UK FCDO advises against all but essential travel within **5 km of the Mexican border on the Guatemalan side**, from the Pacific up to and including Gracias a Dios, citing gang violence; La Mesilla is inside that band. On the Mexican side it advises against all but essential travel **within 40 km of the Guatemalan border in southern Chiapas** (up to Gracias a Dios) and on **Federal Highway 199**, where illegal roadblocks are frequent. This is a secondary summary, dated 2026 — [livinginguatemala.com, La Mesilla guide (blog quoting FCDO), 2026](https://livinginguatemala.com/transport/la-mesilla-border-crossing). Verify on gov.uk.
- US State Department: **Chiapas Level 3, "Reconsider travel"**, due to terrorism and crime. US government employees may not travel to a zone of south-east Chiapas bounded by the Guatemalan border from the Pacific to Netzahualcóyotl and a line from Netzahualcóyotl to Ocosingo. **Tabasco is Level 2, "Exercise increased caution"**, with no employee restrictions — [OSAC/State Dept, "Travel Advisory: Mexico – See State Summaries" [official]](https://www.osac.gov/Country/Mexico/Content/Detail/Report/89194885-afe5-4253-bfab-27eda52cd3cf); [travel.state.gov Mexico advisory [official]](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/mexico-travel-advisory/.html)
- A 2026 blog says Chiapas was raised to Level 3 in Sept 2024 and kept there in the **29 May 2026** reissue, with Mexico overall at Level 2 — [masonthetraveler.com, "Is Chiapas Safe in 2026?" (blog)](https://masonthetraveler.com/post/is-chiapas-safe). Mexico's own guidance for Chiapas was also updated — [Travel And Tour World](https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/mexico-issues-updated-travel-guidance-for-chiapas-as-security-concerns-rise-and-visitors-urged-to-reassess-plans/)
- In **June 2025** a Mexican police operation in La Mesilla left four gunmen dead — [Wikipedia, "2025 in Guatemala"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_in_Guatemala)
- **El Ceibo–Tenosique:**
  - A Nov 2023 human-rights observation mission counted **nine checkpoints** (Army, National Guard, INM) on the El Ceibo–Tenosique–Villahermosa route — [Red TDT / MODH 2023 communiqué](https://redtdt.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Comunicado-MODH2023.pdf)
  - In July 2026 Tenosique was the point where most migrants were arriving, mostly Hondurans, and the El Ceibo–Tenosique stretch had little surveillance — [La Prensa (Honduras), "Tenosique: 61 kilómetros…", July 2026](https://www.laprensa.hn/premium/especiales/tenosique-61-kilometros-via-alterna-menos-conflictiva-mexico-FXLP1304616)
  - Guatemala treats El Ceibo as a point of national-security interest: 12,834 regular crossings Jan–Jul 2024, and blind spots nearby used for people-smuggling — [La Hora (GT), 18 Jul 2024](https://lahora.gt/nacionales/hloarca/2024/07/18/el-ceibo-punto-fronterizo-de-interes-para-la-seguridad-del-pais/)
  - Smugglers use the río San Pedro and wetland channels — [Mexico News Daily](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/news/migrants-find-new-route-into-mexico-via-tabasco-but-also-dangers)
- A Wikipedia article titled "2026 Mexico cartel unrest" exists; contents not retrieved — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mexico_cartel_unrest)

### Inferences
- For cyclists coming from the Guatemalan highlands, **La Mesilla** is the most direct road (CA-1 → Comitán → San Cristóbal). It is also the crossing most clearly inside the official warning zones and the site of the June 2025 violence. Crossing early in the day and riding straight through the 40 km band, without overnighting in Ciudad Cuauhtémoc or Frontera Comalapa, reduces exposure.
- For cyclists coming along the Pacific lowlands (from La Hachadura–Pedro de Alvarado), **El Carmen–Talismán** (quieter) or **Tecún Umán–Ciudad Hidalgo** (busier, fast INM and Banjercito) lead to Tapachula. That area is also inside the US-restricted south-east Chiapas zone and is a major migrant hub, so expect INM and Guardia Nacional checkpoints on the road north. Keep passport, FMM and DNR receipt at hand.
- **El Ceibo–Tenosique** (or the Belize → Chetumal route) avoids Chiapas's Level 3 border zone. It means a long detour through the Petén, a remote road and many checkpoints.
- Bicycles need no Banjercito temporary vehicle import permit (TIP). The vehicle-import advice to use Hidalgo rather than Talismán applies only to motor vehicles.

### Gaps
- Exact INM opening hours at Talismán, Ciudad Cuauhtémoc and El Ceibo in 2026 were not retrieved; the search quota ran out.
- No 2025–2026 reports from cyclists specifically, and none from Colombian cyclists, at these crossings.
- No official confirmation of the FCDO wording; it is quoted via a blog.
- No current information on whether the Bethel–Frontera Corozal boat crossing still has an operating INM post.

## 7. Customs: bicycles, drones, cameras and laptops (focus on Mexico)

### Takeaway
Mexico treats travellers' personal items as duty-free "equipaje" under the RGCE (Reglas Generales de Comercio Exterior) rule 3.2.2, which explicitly lists items such as **two cameras, three mobile phones and one laptop**. Beyond that, the duty-free allowance for other goods is reported as **US$300 by land**. I found no explicit rule on touring bicycles. A 2016 proposal sought to classify bicycles as personal items. Customs officers do inspect bikes at land borders. **Drones** must be declared. For flying, foreigners effectively can't register drones over 250 g with AFAC, because registration requires a Mexican ID or residency. Sub-250 g drones are the practical, if grey-area, option. I found nothing on bike, drone or electronics declaration rules in Guatemala, El Salvador or Honduras.

### Cited Findings
- Passenger baggage ("equipaje") covers personal-use goods. The electronics list includes **two photo or video cameras, photographic material, three portable mobile phones and one laptop** — [Consulmex Francia, "Franquicia pasajeros 2024" (PDF) [official], 2024](https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx/francia/images/pdf/Consulado/franquiciapasajeros2024.pdf); [TLC Asociados, "Franquicia aduanera y equipaje de pasajeros"](https://www.tlcasociados.com.mx/franquicia-aduanera-equipaje-de-pasajeros/)
- The franchise covers both Mexican and foreign passengers. A version of the rule set the allowance for non-baggage goods at **US$300 by land and US$500 by air or sea**; another source cites up to US$500 under rule 3.2.3 — [López Adri, "Regla 3.2.2 Pasajeros"](https://www.lopezadri.com/regla-3-2-2-pasajeros/); [APTA, "Regla 3.2.3"](http://www.apta.com.mx/aptace/reglasce/regla.php?regla=3.2.3); [Miura Ops, "Guide to Passenger Luggage and Duty-Free Allowance in Mexico (2025)"](https://www.miuraops.com/2025/01/27/guide-to-passenger-luggage-and-duty-free-allowance-in-mexico-2025/)
- The franchise does not cover alcohol, tobacco or fuel. Goods above the franchise can be self-declared, paying a flat 16% rate according to one customs broker — [López Adri](https://www.lopezadri.com/regla-3-2-2-pasajeros/). Not verified against the 2026 RGCE.
- An older list mentions "1 caminadora y 1 bicicleta fija" (a treadmill and a stationary bike), not a road or touring bicycle — [transportesyaduanas (blog), 2012](https://transportesyaduanas.wordpress.com/2012/04/24/que-se-considera-equipajes-y-franquicias-de-pasajeros-internacionales-en-mexico/)
- In 2016 there was a proposal to modify rule 3.2.3 so that bicycles are treated as personal articles within the baggage franchise. I could not confirm it was adopted — [Quadratín, "Aerolíneas deberán considerar bicicletas como artículos personales", 2016](https://mexico.quadratin.com.mx/Aerolineas-deberan-considerar-bicicletas-como-articulos-personales)
- Customs inspected the vehicle at Ciudad Hidalgo, Oct 2025 — [terraxplor.com](https://www.terraxplor.com/post/mexico-hidalgo-guatemala)
- **Drones (blogs only; no official AFAC source retrieved):**
  - Most guides say drones over 250 g must be registered with AFAC, and registration requires an official ID proving Mexican citizenship or legal residency, so tourists generally can't register
  - a 2025 guide instead gives a 2 kg threshold for recreational use
  - one source reports an April 2024 AFAC email saying foreign operators cannot fly recreationally at any weight
  - sub-250 g drones are described as a tolerated grey area
  - drones should be declared at customs; sources disagree on duty (one drone per person duty-free, versus duty possible if new or worth over US$300)
  - one source cites fines of up to MXN 400,000 plus confiscation (unverified)

  I could not attribute each claim to a single page among these — [Flying Glass, 2025](https://www.flyingglass.com.au/drone-laws-in-mexico/); [DronesGator, 2026](https://dronesgator.com/drone-laws-in-mexico); [UAVWiki, 2026](https://uavwiki.com/mexico-drone-laws-2026-complete-guide-for-tourists-afac-registration-2kg-threshold-mayan-ruins-no-fly-real-fines/); [Mexique Aventure, 2026](https://mexiqueaventure.com/en/can-you-use-your-drone-in-mexico-mexique-aventures-complete-guide/); [Fitcation HQ, 2026](https://fitcationhq.com/mexico-drone-regulations-for-tourists-2026-guide/); [TS2, 2025](https://ts2.tech/en/mexico-drone-laws-2025-regulations-requirements-and-recent-updates/); [Loco Gringo](https://www.locogringo.com/blog/vacation-tips/electronics-and-drone-regulations-when-traveling-mexico)

### Inferences
- Bikes ridden in, clearly used and loaded with touring gear will very likely pass as personal baggage or sports equipment. No permit is needed, unlike motor vehicles. Carrying purchase receipts or photos of the frame serial numbers helps if customs questions value or ownership.
- Each rider should keep within the explicit baggage list: up to two cameras, three phones and one laptop per person. Extra electronics, such as a second laptop or a new drone, risk duty above the US$300 land franchise.
- If they carry a drone, a sub-250 g model, declared at customs and flown conservatively (no archaeological sites or protected areas, no towns), is the least risky option. A drone over 250 g should be treated as legally unflyable for tourists in Mexico.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the 2026 RGCE text, so there is no confirmation of the current franchise amounts or of whether a bicycle or "equipo deportivo" is explicitly listed as baggage.
- No official AFAC statement on foreign tourists' drones was retrieved.
- Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras and Belize customs rules on bicycles, drones, cameras and laptops: no sources found.
- The yellow-fever certificate requirement (for travellers from Colombia) appeared only in a 2018 agency document; health requirements are presumably covered by another researcher.
